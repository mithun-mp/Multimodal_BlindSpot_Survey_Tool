## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

An extensive, read-only audit of the entire BlindSpot code repository, run records (`runs/`), and persistent caches (`cache/semantic_cache.json`) has revealed a multi-layer logical and architectural failure in how semantic expectations, ground-truth references, and model failures are calculated and displayed.

The contradictory UI state observed:
- **Baseline**: `NEUTRAL`, `Confidence: 75%`, `Final Ground-Truth: NEUTRAL`, `Source: MANUAL`
- **Probe**: `"The king is not injured on his left leg"`, `Probe semantic: NEGATIVE`, `Confidence: 75%`, `Relation: SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL`
- **Probe Catalog Card**: `REVERSE_POLARITY`, `EXPECTS FLIP`, `Rationale: Linguistic negation reversing truth-conditional semantic polarity.`
- **Gemini Status Card**: `Provider: Manual`, `Model: manual`, `Offline (Manual Mode)`, `Requests: 0`, `Failures: 0`, `Retries: 0`

is **NOT an external API bug with Google Gemini**. Gemini was **never called** (0 requests made).

Instead, this state is the composite consequence of **five distinct, compounding system faults**:

1. **Conflation of Factual Truth-Condition Reversal with Sentiment Polarity Reversal**:  
   The probe generator (`blindspot/perturbations/shared.py`, lines 374–386) unconditionally assumes that any grammatical negation operator (`not`, `never`, `n't`) constitutes a **sentiment reversal** (`expected_flip=True`, `semantic_intent=REVERSE_POLARITY`). While negating "injured" alters the proposition's factual truth value, it does **not** convert the sentence into negative sentiment. Negating an injury ("not injured") expresses either an objective factual absence of injury (`NEUTRAL`) or relief (`POSITIVE`), never `NEGATIVE`.

2. **Arbitrary Hardcoded Confidence Fallback Masquerading as Calibrated Certainty**:  
   The `0.75` (75%) confidence displayed in the UI does not come from Gemini, a neural network, or statistical calibration. It was hardcoded in `blindspot/semantic/service.py` as a manual fallback constant when `is_online == False`. Furthermore, `SemanticAnnotation.__post_init__` in `blindspot/semantic/types.py` silently copies `self.confidence` into `self.gemini_confidence`, creating persistent JSON entries in `cache/semantic_cache.json` falsely recording `"gemini_confidence": 0.75` for runs where Gemini was never invoked.

3. **Competing and Contradictory Expectation Engines**:  
   There is no single source of truth for probe expectations. The probe generator (`shared.py`) generates `expected_flip=True` and `semantic_intent=REVERSE_POLARITY`. The semantic reference set (`types.py`) computes `relation=SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL`. The execution runner (`runner.py`, line 235) forces `SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL` to become `REVERSE_POLARITY` and `expected_flip=True`. The behavioral engine (`behavioral.py`) resolves `exp_type` independently and falls into an `UNDETERMINED` or `BLIND` trap when evaluating neutral baselines.

4. **Misleading Nomenclature ("Final Ground-Truth")**:  
   The UI renders `final_semantic_polarity` with the label `Final Ground-Truth: NEUTRAL`, even when the label was generated by a rudimentary heuristic fallback without human verification.

5. **Contamination of Diagnostic Failure Taxonomy (False BLIND / SPURIOUS Classifications)**:  
   Because `runner.py` and `shared.py` demand `REVERSE_POLARITY` on sentences that have no sentiment reversal, a robust model (such as Twitter-RoBERTa predicting `NEUTRAL → NEUTRAL`) is penalized and falsely classified as **`BLIND`** or **`UNDETERMINED`**. Conversely, a model predicting an unjustified flip is credited with `EXPECTED_FLIP`.

## 7. CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC CHECK: FACTUAL TRUTH-CONDITION VS. SENTIMENT POLARITY

This is the core conceptual bug in the BlindSpot research design.

### The Formal Distinction:
- **Propositional Truth Conditions (Formal Semantics)**:  
  Let $P$ be the proposition "The king is injured on his left leg".  
  In modal logic: $\nu(P) \in \{0, 1\}$.  
  The negation operator $\neg P$ ("The king is not injured on his left leg") guarantees $\nu(\neg P) = 1 - \nu(P)$.  
  This is a **truth-conditional inversion**.

- **Sentiment Polarity (Evaluative Affective Semantics)**:  
  Sentiment models do not predict truth conditions; they predict affective stance, valence, or tone: $\sigma(S) \in \{\text{NEGATIVE}, \text{NEUTRAL}, \text{POSITIVE}\}$.  
  Negating a factual proposition does **not** map homomorphically to sentiment negation:
  
  $$\neg P \not\Rightarrow \sigma(\neg P) = -\sigma(P)$$

### Concrete Analysis of the King Example:
1. `"The king is injured on his left leg"`:  
   - In news, medical, or historical reporting, this is an objective factual report: $\sigma = \text{NEUTRAL}$.  
   - In clinical or affective analysis, suffering an injury is an adverse event: $\sigma = \text{NEGATIVE}$.
2. `"The king is not injured on his left leg"`:  
   - In news reporting, it reports a factual state of health: $\sigma = \text{NEUTRAL}$.  
   - In affective analysis, relief from injury is either $\text{NEUTRAL}$ or mildly $\text{POSITIVE}$.  
   - **Under no valid linguistic theory is "The king is not injured" classified as NEGATIVE!**

Yet the old manual fallback produced:
- Baseline: `NEUTRAL`
- Probe: `NEGATIVE`
- Relation: `SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL`
while the probe catalog declared:
- Intent: `REVERSE_POLARITY`
- Expectation: `EXPECTS FLIP`

This is completely contradictory. Reversing `NEUTRAL` is undefined in binary polarity, and asserting that removing an injury creates negative sentiment is factually and linguistically false.

## 10. MULTIPLE COMPETING EXPECTATION CALCULATIONS (ARCHITECTURAL DEFECT)

The audit revealed that probe expectations are calculated in **seven separate, uncoordinated locations** across the codebase:

```text
[1] LinguisticProbeCatalog (shared.py:375)
    → Sets probe.expected_flip = True, semantic_intent = REVERSE_POLARITY

[2] infer_expected_semantic_effect (shared.py:106)
    → Sets ("invert", True) based purely on string matching "negation"

[3] SemanticRelation.from_polarities (types.py:78)
    → Calculates SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL from (NEUTRAL, NEGATIVE)

[4] SemanticReferenceService._infer_probe_polarity (service.py:140)
    → Infers probe polarity based on expected_flip and text cues

[5] Execution Runner (runner.py:235-239)
    → OVERWRITES probe.expected_flip = True, semantic_intent = REVERSE_POLARITY whenever rel_val in ("REVERSE", "SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL")

[6] Behavioral Classifier (behavioral.py:143-156)
    → Derives exp_type from intent/effect, then resolves exp_rel from orig_pol

[7] UI Probe Catalog (probe_lab.py:158)
    → Displays "EXPECTS FLIP" directly from probe.expected_flip
```

### The Conflict:
Because Runner ([5]) forcibly overwrites the probe's `expected_flip` to `True` when `rel_val == "SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL"`, the system obliterates the distinction between a true polarity reversal (`POSITIVE ↔ NEGATIVE`) and a shift to/from neutral (`NEUTRAL → NEGATIVE`).

## 11. BINARY VS. THREE-CLASS MODEL EVALUATION AUDIT

### 2-Class Model Audit (SST-2: DistilBERT, ALBERT, BERT Base):
- **Label Space**: Strictly `{"POSITIVE", "NEGATIVE"}`.
- **The Problem**: There is no native `NEUTRAL` class.
- When given `"The king is injured on his left leg"`:
  - The model is forced to choose between `POSITIVE` and `NEGATIVE`.
  - SST-2 models overwhelmingly predict `NEGATIVE` (due to the token "injured").
- When given `"The king is not injured on his left leg"`:
  - If the model lacks compositional negation reasoning, it focuses on "injured" and predicts `NEGATIVE` again (`NEGATIVE → NEGATIVE`).
  - Because `runner.py` declared `expected_flip=True` and `DIFFERENT_LABEL`:
  - `behavioral.py` checks `raw_flipped == False`.
  - **The model is classified as `BLIND` (Failure)**.
  - However, if the model flips to `POSITIVE` (`NEGATIVE → POSITIVE`), the system credits it with `EXPECTED_FLIP` (Success). But "The king is not injured on his left leg" is an objective factual statement, not a positive sentiment! The model is being rewarded for a hallucinated positive sentiment!

### 3-Class Model Audit (Twitter-RoBERTa Latest & Base):
- **Label Space**: `{"POSITIVE", "NEUTRAL", "NEGATIVE"}`.
- **The Problem**:
  - Twitter-RoBERTa correctly classifies both statements as `NEUTRAL` (`NEUTRAL → NEUTRAL`).
  - Because `runner.py` declared `expected_flip=True` and `DIFFERENT_LABEL`:
  - The model did not change its label (`raw_flipped == False`).
  - `behavioral.py` falls through to lines 215–222 or line 312:
  - **The model is classified as `BLIND` or `UNDETERMINED`!**
  - A highly accurate model that correctly recognizes neutral factual language is penalized as defective!

## 12. IMPACT ON DIAGNOSTIC FAILURE TAXONOMY

The 4-way behavioral failure taxonomy (`BLIND`, `SPURIOUS`, `MISWEIGHTED`, `UNDETERMINED`) relies directly on the integrity of the probe expectation contract.

| Failure Category | Intended Scientific Meaning | How It Is Currently Falsely Triggered |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`BLIND`** | Model is insensitive to a genuine meaning-altering perturbation (e.g. fails to flip when sentiment truly reversed). | Falsely triggered whenever a model correctly maintains its prediction on a factual negation where no sentiment flip actually occurred (e.g. `NEUTRAL → NEUTRAL` on "not injured"). |
| **`SPURIOUS`** | Model flips its prediction in response to an invariant, meaning-preserving perturbation. | Falsely triggered if an appended contrast clause genuinely shifted discourse sentiment, but the probe catalog labeled it `PRESERVE_MEANING`. |
| **`MISWEIGHTED`** | Model confidence moves in the opposite direction of modifier intent (e.g. intensifier causes confidence collapse). | Falsely triggered when a probe shifts toward neutral and confidence drops legitimately, but the system expects rigid polarity preservation. |
| **`UNDETERMINED`**| Probe contract is ambiguous or model outputs are malformed. | Falsely triggered when `orig_pol == NEUTRAL` and `exp_type == REVERSE_POLARITY`, causing `resolve_expected_behavioral_relation` to return `OTHER`, which drops through the decision tree into `UNDETERMINED`. |

**Conclusion**: The current semantic expectation logic directly corrupts the scientific validity of the failure taxonomy.

Inspection of `blindspot/semantic/cache.py` (lines 37–40):
```python
def _compute_key(self, sentence_text: str) -> str:
    norm = self._normalize(sentence_text)
    raw = f"{norm}|{self.annotation_version}"
    return hashlib.sha256(raw.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()
```

### Critical Findings:
1. **Cache Key Missing Engine & Model Identity**:  
   The cache key is computed solely from `normalized_sentence_text` and `annotation_version` (`"v1.0"`).
   It does **NOT** include:
   - `annotation_engine` (`"gemini"` vs `"manual_fallback"` vs `"antigravity_exact"`),
   - `model_name` (`"gemini-2.5-flash"` vs `"manual"`),
   - `schema_version`.
2. **Persistent Stale Contamination**:  
   When the King sentences were evaluated under the naive manual fallback, entries with `"confidence": 0.75` and `"model_used": "manual_fallback"` were written to `cache/semantic_cache.json`.
   Any subsequent run—even if Gemini is enabled, or an improved exact analyzer is deployed—looks up the hash `24014b91...` and **hits the stale cache**, returning the faulty `NEGATIVE (75%)` annotation!

## 15. ANSWERS TO MANDATORY ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA (SECTION 36)

1. **Did Gemini actually run?**  
   **NO.** Gemini was never called.
2. **If not, why not?**  
   `GEMINI_API_KEY` was not configured in the host environment (`client.is_online == False`). The service bypassed the API call block.
3. **Where did 75% come from?**  
   It was an arbitrary hardcoded constant (`0.75`) in the manual fallback constructor of `service.py`, copied into `gemini_confidence` by `types.py:140`.
4. **Where did NEUTRAL come from?**  
   From the rudimentary fallback keyword search, which lacked the token `"injured"` in its dictionary and defaulted to `NEUTRAL`.
5. **Where did NEGATIVE come from?**  
   From the old `_infer_probe_polarity()` heuristic, which saw `expected_flip=True` on `P001` and arbitrarily flipped `NEUTRAL` to `NEGATIVE`.
6. **Where did SHIFT_FROM_NEUTRAL come from?**  
   From `SemanticRelation.from_polarities(NEUTRAL, NEGATIVE)` in `types.py:97`.
7. **Where did REVERSE_POLARITY come from?**  
   From the hardcoded slot configuration in `LinguisticProbeCatalog.generate_candidates()` (`shared.py:377`).
8. **Where did EXPECTS FLIP come from?**  
   From `expected_flip = True` hardcoded for slot `P001` in `shared.py:375`, rendered by `probe_lab.py:158`.
9. **Are those calculations independent or duplicated?**  
   They are duplicated, competing, and uncoordinated across 7 different files.
10. **Is truth-condition reversal being confused with sentiment reversal?**  
    **YES.** Grammatical negation was treated as guaranteed sentiment polarity reversal.
11. **Is the probe generator assuming every negation changes sentiment?**  
    **YES.** `infer_expected_semantic_effect` and `P001` unconditionally set `expected_flip = True` for all negation probes.
12. **Are binary models being evaluated correctly?**  
    **NO.** Binary models cannot represent `NEUTRAL` and are falsely flagged as `BLIND` when they do not flip on non-sentiment negations.
13. **Are three-class models being evaluated correctly?**  
    **NO.** Three-class models predicting `NEUTRAL → NEUTRAL` are penalized as `BLIND` because the system expects a flip.
14. **Can incorrect semantic expectations create false BLIND / SPURIOUS / MISWEIGHTED failures?**  
    **YES.** False failures are directly manufactured when empirical predictions disagree with flawed semantic expectations.
15. **Can manual fallback values masquerade as Gemini results?**  
    **YES.** `types.py` defaulted `gemini_polarity` and `gemini_confidence` to manual fallback values and wrote them to cache.
16. **Is human verification authoritative?**  
    The data model supports human overrides, but the execution runner overwrote `expected_flip` dynamically, and unverified annotations were labeled "Ground-Truth".
17. **Is every semantic decision traceable?**  
    Traceable through JSON files, but obscured by false `gemini_confidence` entries.
18. **Can every displayed result be reproduced from stored data?**  
    Yes, verified via `cache/semantic_cache.json` and `runs/exp_1790545764_7312bc/semantic_reference.json`.
